<objective>
Make the multimodal beat appear on every chat claim, and make every chat claim leave a
photo-carrying artifact behind.

Two gaps, both found by running the live public demo on 2026-08-13. **(1)** The photo is only
requested on the cracked-screen path, so the two highest-value claims - $3,000 total loss and
liquid damage - settle on the customer's word while an $840 screen repair requires evidence.
**(2)** The photo is assessed and then discarded: it attaches only to the `[ASSESSOR]` packet,
which fires only on escalation, so on the common auto-approved path the image drives the
decision and is then lost. Live proof of gap 2: conversation `dfMessenger-3b9ce119`
(2026-08-13T22:52:23Z, chat v15 `129f8b31`, `PDP100294`) - photo uploaded, `CRACK_CONFIRMED`,
`CLM-24548` auto-approved at $840, `record_claim` recorded, customer email sent, **no packet,
no attachment, image gone.**

Gap 2 defeats the stated purpose. The user intends to feed claim photos to a Gemini Enterprise
agent that reviews them against a policy-compliance document. That needs a corpus of every
claim, not just the exceptions. Today the only route to a photo-carrying artifact is the
photo-contradiction path, which is the rarest branch and has never been run live on any channel.

Purpose: every chat claim asks for a photo; every resolved chat claim files a record email that
carries it; the two dispositions are triageable from the subject line.
Output: chat instruction + callback edits, one new tool, one edited mailer, a version cut, a
gated repoint, and a SUMMARY.

**Chat only.** Voice is audio-only and keeps asking for photos by email reply. **Do not change
voice.** Out of scope: the deterministic English strings (05-09 owns those), the Resend key
rotation, and `resolve_claim`'s truthfulness fix (260812-trc, pending separately).
</objective>

<platform_constraints>
Every one of these is proven on this project and every one of them will bite an executor that
ignores it. They are not background reading - they are the reason each task is shaped the way
it is.

1. **The uploaded image exists in `context.user_content.parts[].inline_data` for exactly ONE
   turn** and is absent from conversation history on every later turn (260812-o5l SPIKE, run
   live, three turns, tabulated). `Blob.data` is *already base64* - hand it to Resend verbatim,
   never re-encode. It must be captured on the upload turn and carried in session variables.
2. **A single session variable may not exceed 262,144 bytes.** An oversize write returns
   `HTTP 400 FAILED_PRECONDITION` from `runSession` itself and **kills the customer's entire
   turn** - not a soft error the tool can swallow. Slice at 250,000 and refuse outright above
   4,000,000 base64 chars, writing nothing. The guard sits BEFORE the write.
3. **A `beforeToolCallback` that returns a dict short-circuits the tool before its body runs.**
   This is why `assess_screen_crack` could never capture a photo volunteered mid-diagnostic, and
   why its gate was re-keyed from `dx_outcome` to `issue_category`. In this plan that mechanism
   is used deliberately, as the refusal for non-screen claims.
4. **A widget tool call carrying a `textResponse` TERMINATES the model turn.** Since v9 the
   decision card *is* the decision text, so anything that must run on the decision turn goes
   BEFORE the card - that is why the order is `resolve_claim` -> `record_claim` ->
   `claim_decision_card` (06-03, cost two conversations to learn).
5. **Turn placement is load-bearing and has failed twice.** The packet had to move to the
   email-confirmation turn because a later turn only fires if the customer speaks again; and
   moving instruction text silenced a whole turn in chat v11 `838b6d2b`. Any new tool call must
   have its turn chosen deliberately and its customer-facing text asserted unchanged.
6. **The last paragraph of a step wins** (o5l "recency trap"): the same byte-identical rule
   produced opposite defects on the approve and escalated paths depending only on where it sat.
   The `"Ask for photos by email"` step must still END on *"must not be the decision alone."*
   **Never append a paragraph after a step's closing rule.**
7. **`cover_offer_actions` can be broken by wording elsewhere.** Closing the send-away line's
   open loop made the model emit `end_session` and kill the cross-sell. Re-run the cross-sell
   canary after ANY closing-wording change; a control against the live deployment via
   `config.deployment` costs nothing and settles it.
8. **A Resend 200 proves acceptance, not delivery.** Emails have been landing in spam. Any
   delivery claim needs an inbox check by the user.
9. **Tool code changes are not effective immediately** - wait ~25 s after `tools.patch` before
   invoking. Read-back does NOT prove the runtime picked it up.
10. **A tool that swallows its own exceptions cannot be verified by reading it** (05-07's
    `timeout=20`, which meant the voice mailer had never delivered a single email). Every change
    to `send_case_record_email` is proven by one live invocation.
11. **CES tool authoring rules** (06-02): exactly ONE module-level `def` or the tool is named
    after the first helper; every parameter needs an explicit type annotation; a default may not
    be `None`; there is no `inputSchema` and `description` is overwritten by the docstring;
    `ces_requests` is an injected global, `import ces_requests` raises `ModuleNotFoundError`;
    branch on `status_code`, never on `.ok`.
12. **Edit method, non-negotiable:** scripted `str.replace()` **from a file**, anchors sliced out
    of the resource's own bytes, uniqueness asserted before, a delta bound stated before running,
    contiguity measured by common prefix/suffix, byte-for-byte read-back after. Chat
    `claim_intake` is **pure LF**; chat `claims_concierge` is **CRLF** - anchors are not
    transferable even within one app. Print booleans and lengths, never source, on any
    key-bearing tool.
13. **Quota:** ~120-150k input tokens per CES conversation. Prefer `apps.executeTool` (no
    conversation, no quota) wherever it answers the question. Pace conversations >=75 s apart;
    on a 429 use exactly ONE spaced retry, never a loop.
14. **Live state:** chat `d7bfbb93` serves **v15 `129f8b31-f06e-48cc-90fb-15dcf8611db1`**. The
    rollback target must be **re-read live from the API immediately before the PATCH**, never
    taken from this document. Voice `d28bbcb0` serves v14 `5d02f14c` and must end this plan
    unchanged.
</platform_constraints>

<positions>
These are decided, with reasons. An executor implements them; it does not re-litigate them.

**P1 - A customer with no photo is never blocked, and the record says so.**
The agent asks once, in the same turn it acknowledges the loss. If the customer says they cannot
send one, or simply answers the next diagnostic question instead, the agent proceeds immediately
and the claim resolves exactly as it does today - same tariff, same decision, same card, same
customer email. There is no second ask, no "are you sure", no gate on the decision. Blocking a
$3,000 total-loss claim on an image the customer may not be able to take would be a worse demo
than not asking at all. The record email tells the truth about it with a `PHOTO: attached` /
`PHOTO: not provided` line derived from the **mailer's own `attached` boolean** - the one place
in the system that actually knows. It is NOT derived from `photo_status`, which o5l deliberately
left writing `"requested"` and which 05-05 had a collision on; that variable and the whole
`case_summary` packet composer stay byte-unchanged.

**P2 - The extra turn is worth it.**
Yes, it adds a beat to the total-loss and liquid-damage paths, which today skip it. Take it. The
multimodal upload is the single most-cited moment in Google's own launch materials ("a photo of
a damaged appliance" is their flagship CX example), and it currently appears on exactly one of
three demo scenarios - and on the cheapest one. A demo that shows vision only for an $840 screen
is a demo that shows vision on the claim where it matters least. Cost is mitigated by *where*
the ask goes: it rides along with the acknowledgement turn the agent already takes, so it costs
the customer one extra utterance, not one extra exchange. If a presenter wants the short path,
they decline the photo and lose nothing.

**P3 - An email per claim is affordable, with one rule.**
Resend free tier is 100/day and 3,000/month; headers observed on 2026-08-12 read ~21/100 used
and ~92/3,000, and those are *used* counts. Today a claim costs ~1.15 sends (1 customer email
always, 1 packet only on escalation). After this change it costs exactly 2. A 20-claim demo day
goes from ~23 to 40 sends - comfortably clear. The risk is not the demo, it is the build: 21
sends were burned in a single test day, and a build-plus-rehearse day could reach 60-80. So the
rule: **`send_case_record_email` returns `x-resend-daily-quota` in its result and the executor
reports it in the SUMMARY**, and no canary battery runs more than 10 live conversations in a
day. Note that `apps.executeTool` saves *conversation* quota, not Resend quota - an executeTool
invocation of the mailer really sends. The pending key rotation should provision a verified
sending domain, which independently fixes emails landing in spam.

**P4 - `[RECORD]` is the right token, and the corpus filter is the CHANNEL token, not the
leading one.**
Subjects ship as `[ASSESSOR] [CHAT] CLM-… - Jordan Rivera` (byte-unchanged, escalation) and
`[RECORD] [CHAT] CLM-… - Jordan Rivera - APPROVED` (new, auto-approve). Reasoning: the leading
token is what a **human** triages on, and `[ASSESSOR]` already means "a person must act" - so
appending `- ACTION REQUIRED` to it, as the brief proposed, adds no information and does add
risk, because that subject is live, documented in DEMO-RUNBOOK.md, and proven since 05-07. It
stays byte-identical. The **downstream Gemini Enterprise agent filters on `[CHAT]` / `[VOICE]`**,
which is already stamped on every packet on both channels and will now be on every
record-carrying email - that is the stable corpus selector, and it survives any future addition
of a third disposition. Disposition is then readable from the leading token without opening the
mail. ASCII only, per 05-07's channel-token decision; no em dash.

**P5 - The vision tool is refused deterministically on non-screen claims, not asked politely.**
A total-loss device is inert; asking a vision model to find damage it cannot see invites exactly
the contamination that killed the wrong-subject guard, which this project has already proven
**unfixable by prompting** (commit 2c22582). So `assess_screen_crack` is gated OFF for
non-cracked-screen claims in `claim_intake`'s `beforeToolCallback` - the same short-circuit
mechanism that refused it three times in a row in o5l. The image is still asked for, still
captured, still attached; it is simply never assessed. Capture therefore cannot live inside
`assess_screen_crack` on those branches (a short-circuited tool's body never runs), which is why
`capture_claim_photo` exists as a separate, vision-free tool.
</positions>
