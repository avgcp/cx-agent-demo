## Current shape of the code being changed

`assess_screen_crack(crack_visible, keyboard_visible, hinged_lid, what_you_see)`:

1. Validates three enums.
2. Increments `photo_attempts`, stores `photo_observation`.
3. **Infers the device** from `keyboard_visible` / `hinged_lid` / keyword-matching
   `what_you_see`, writes `photo_device_seen`, compares against `device_category` from the
   policy record, and returns `WRONG_SUBJECT` with `photo_check="rejected"` on mismatch.
   ← **this is what comes out**
4. `crack == "unclear"` **or** `inferred == "unclear"` → one retry, then `HUMAN_REVIEW` / DL-5.
5. `crack == "no"` → `photo_contradiction = True`, `photo_check="contradicted"`, DL-5,
   non-accusatory wording. ← **must survive**
6. Otherwise `CRACK_CONFIRMED`, `photo_check="confirmed"`, proceed to `resolve_claim`.

**Answering the open question in the brief:** there is no identity-independent rejection
branch to preserve. A photo with no device or no screen in it is already handled by
`crack_visible="unclear"` (its enum text is *"too blurry, too dark, badly framed, or the
screen is not in shot"*) → retry → human. Step 3 is the only device-identity logic, and it
goes in full. The `inferred == "unclear"` disjunct in step 4 goes with it; step 4 then rests
on `crack_visible` alone.

Downstream consumers of `photo_check`, **both left functionally unchanged**:
- `tools/resolve_claim/…/python_code.py` — `photo_blocked = photo in ("contradicted","unclear","rejected")` forces `HUMAN_REVIEW`.
- `agents/claim_intake/before_tool_callbacks/…/python_code.py` — gates `resolve_claim` until `photo_check` is one of `confirmed|contradicted|unclear|rejected`.

`"rejected"` simply becomes unreachable in both. Leave the value in place (defensive, costs
nothing); add one comment line in `resolve_claim` saying so.
