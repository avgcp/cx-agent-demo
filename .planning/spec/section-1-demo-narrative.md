## Demo Narrative / Storyline

A single, ≤10–15 minute, single-sitting, single-line-of-business (personal device/gadget insurance) white-label demo run. One continuous conversation, one claimant, one carrier — every locked wow moment is sequenced into this one story rather than shown as isolated feature demos.

### Beat-by-Beat Script

1. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Chat FNOL intake opens.** Carrier: Meridian Device Protection. Claimant Jordan Rivera opens a chat conversation with the "Claims Concierge" agent. The agent greets, asks for name and policy ID, and verifies the policy: `claimant_name` = Jordan Rivera, `policy_id` = PDP-100294. *[Reuses V1 happy-path intake.]*

2. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Coverage / deductible lookup.** The agent looks up the policy and states the canonical coverage terms: deductible $100 (`deductible`, Policy PDP-100294) and coverage limit $3,500 per device (`coverage_limit`, Policy PDP-100294). Both figures are returned by a structured Python tool call against the mock policy record — never free-generated. *[Reuses V1 happy-path coverage lookup.]*

3. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Claim 1 (small): photo of a cracked screen.** Jordan uploads a photo of a cracked laptop screen (`damage_image_ref` = IMG-CRACK-01). The agent runs native multimodal damage assessment on the image and returns: moderate cosmetic screen damage, repairable, LOW severity. The agent opens claim CLM-24001 and states the estimate: **$450** (`claim_amount`, CLM-24001).

4. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Decision branch — autonomous.** The agent applies **Rule DL-1** (`claim_amount ≤ $1,000 AND coverage_valid = true AND total_loss_flag = false`) against CLM-24001 ($450, coverage valid, not total-loss) and auto-approves the claim, issuing a claim number. The agent **visibly states which rule it applied and why** — e.g., "This claim is $450, under our $1,000 auto-approval threshold, with no total-loss flag — I'm applying Rule DL-1 and auto-approving. This account's policy routes claims under $1,000 with no total-loss flag to auto-approval; thresholds are configurable per line of business." This is the "make the decision visible" moment (UX pitfall guardrail): the audience sees the rule reference, not a silent outcome.

5. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Voice + mid-demo language switch (EN↔ES US).** Jordan switches to the voice channel to report a second loss and says (verbatim, per the Multilingual Test Phrases in Section 4): *"Hi, I dropped my laptop and the screen is cracked."* — establishing the voice channel is live. Mid-conversation, Jordan says (verbatim trigger phrase): **"¿Podemos continuar en español?"** The agent detects the language switch and continues the conversation in Spanish, confirming with the Spanish FNOL phrase context: *"Se me cayó la laptop y la pantalla está rota."* is understood and the agent responds in kind (per D-10). If live audio-to-audio translation is not confirmed in-console at build time, the agent falls back to a **captioned-text** rendering of the same exchange (per D-11) rather than asserting spoken live-translation as guaranteed.

6. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Claim 2 (large), back-to-back: liquid-damaged high-end laptop.** Jordan uploads a photo of a liquid-damaged laptop (`damage_image_ref` = IMG-LIQUID-01). Damage assessment returns: severe internal liquid damage, likely total loss, HIGH severity. The agent opens claim CLM-24002 and states the estimate: **$2,400** (`claim_amount`, CLM-24002), with `total_loss_flag = true` and `data_loss_flag = true`.

7. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Empathy / tone adaptation (ADD-01, D-06).** Jordan expresses genuine distress about losing work files and irreplaceable data on the destroyed machine. The agent visibly detects this distress (word choice, expressed concern about data loss) and adapts its tone — slower pacing, acknowledging language ("I'm sorry — losing your work like that is really frustrating, let's get this sorted for you") — before proceeding to escalation. This is the same moment that carries the empathy wow moment, per D-06.

8. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Decision branch — human-in-the-loop (HITL).** The agent applies **Rule DL-2** (`claim_amount > $1,000` — $2,400 exceeds the $1,000 ceiling) and **Rule DL-3** (`total_loss_flag = true` — always routes to a human regardless of amount) against CLM-24002. Either rule alone is sufficient; both fire. The agent **visibly states the routing decision and cites both rule IDs**: "This claim is $2,400, over our $1,000 threshold, and it's flagged as a total loss — per Rules DL-2 and DL-3, I'm routing this to a human assessor rather than auto-approving." The agent then demonstrates a **warm, context-rich handoff** — not a dead end: it confirms Jordan's case, claim details, and conversation context are already packaged for the assessor, and gives an expected-response timeframe, proving HITL is a supported path, not a failure state.

9. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Backend claims-processing reveal — the "screen behind the screen."** The presenter switches to a second view showing the backend claims-processing chain: the full conversation transcript feeds a specialist-summary sub-agent, which produces (a) for the autonomous CLM-24001 claim, an **auto-drafted customer email** confirming the approval, claim number, and next steps; and (b) for the HITL CLM-24002 claim, an **assessor briefing packet** — a structured case summary (claimant, loss description, photos, distress signals noted, applicable rule citations) ready for the human assessor to pick up with full context. This is spec'd as a custom in-session sub-agent + Python tool chain operating on the transcript and structured claim data — not an assumed off-the-shelf Gemini Enterprise ingestion pipeline (per CAP-02 Open Questions, Section 2).

10. **[DETERMINISTIC — must land]** **Cross-sell / upsell close (D-08, ADD-04).** With both claims resolved, the agent applies **Rule DL-4** (`claim resolved AND profile has uninsured_device`). Jordan's seeded profile shows a recently purchased smartphone that is not currently on any policy (`uninsured_device`). The agent notes this personalized detail and offers a device-bundle protection plan covering the phone alongside the laptop — the "cost center → profit center" closing beat, made to feel earned because it's tied to a specific detail about Jordan's account rather than a generic upsell pitch.

11. **[FLEX — presenter may improvise]** Throughout the run, the presenter may genuinely improvise small talk, ask the agent a rephrased or slightly off-script version of a question, or vary pacing — proving the agent is generative, not a fixed script.

12. **[CURVEBALL]** At any natural point (commonly after Beat 2 or Beat 6), the presenter asks the agent a deliberately out-of-scope question unrelated to the claim — e.g., "what's the weather like where you are?" or "can you also help me update my auto policy?" — and the agent gracefully deflects/redirects back to the FNOL task without breaking character or hallucinating an answer. This is the scripted "prove it's not scripted" moment (Pitfall 11): it demonstrates genuine generative flexibility rather than a rigid, deck-like walkthrough.

**Demo ceiling:** the full run — both claims, the language switch, the empathy beat, both decision branches, the backend reveal, and the cross-sell close — is scripted to fit a single ≤10–15 minute sitting.

### What V2 Adds Over V1

**V1 baseline (already built):** a chat-only, single-language FNOL happy path — agent "Alex" greets the claimant, collects name + policy ID, returns coverage/deductible, requests a photo of the damaged item, runs multimodal damage analysis, and returns a claim number. V1 also demonstrates one escalation/HITL branch (injury) and one out-of-scope deflection, but shows only ONE claim, ONE channel, ONE language, and no backend-processing visibility or cross-sell.

**V2 adds, layered on top of that same reusable intake spine:**
- A **voice channel**, not just chat, with a **mid-demo English↔Spanish (US) language switch** — the customer can start or continue the conversation by voice and switch languages naturally mid-call.
- The **visible autonomous-vs-human decision branch, shown BOTH ways in the same run** — not just claimed as a capability, but demonstrated back-to-back with two claims that land on opposite sides of the threshold, with the agent stating which rule it applied each time.
- **Sentiment/empathy tone adaptation** — the agent detects genuine customer distress (data loss on the total-loss claim) and visibly adapts its tone before escalating, addressing the "AI has no empathy" objection directly.
- The **backend claims-processing reveal** — the "screen behind the screen" showing the transcript-to-specialist-summary-to-drafted-artifact chain for both the auto-approved claim (customer email) and the escalated claim (assessor briefing packet) — proving the system does the specialist's paperwork, not just the customer-facing chat.
- A **cross-sell / upsell close** — the "cost center → profit center" moment, personalized to a specific gap in Jordan's coverage, closing the demo on a revenue argument rather than stopping at claim resolution.

### White-Label & Sector Generalization

The carrier (Meridian Device Protection) and the specific product (personal device/gadget insurance, laptop claims) are **generic, unbranded, swappable placeholders** — not a real insurer and not tied to any single account. The run itself generalizes directly to **any personal-lines / device / gadget / contents insurer**, and the underlying pattern generalizes further to **broader P&C FNOL lines — auto, home, and property** — anywhere a claim has a dollar amount, a coverage record, a photo-assessable loss, and a potential coverage gap to cross-sell against.

What is **structural and reusable** across every account/sector: the decision-branch pattern (deterministic threshold + always-escalate flag), the escalation/HITL handoff pattern, the cross-sell trigger pattern (resolved claim + a detected coverage gap), and the backend-reveal pattern (transcript → summary → drafted artifact). What is **illustrative and swappable** per account: the specific loss type (laptop screen/liquid damage), the specific cross-sell product (device bundle), and the carrier name/branding.

Per the "one line of business per run" discipline (D-01), this demo run stays **single-sector** — device/gadget insurance only, no multi-sector branching live on stage. Multi-sector reach is demonstrated by documenting the swap points above, not by showing multiple verticals in one sitting (a 10–15 minute demo showing auto AND home AND pet in one run would dilute every wow moment rather than landing any of them).
